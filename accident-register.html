49 CFR § 390.15 — Accident Register Requirements

Every motor carrier subject to the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations must maintain a register of accidents involving its commercial motor vehicles. The register must be retained at the motor carrier's principal place of business for three years after the date of each accident, and must be made available to any authorized Federal, State, or local official.

🔗 View 49 CFR § 390.15 on eCFR.gov

What Must Be Recorded

For each accident, the register must include:

  • Date of the accident
  • City or town, or most near, and State in which the accident occurred
  • Driver name
  • Number of injuries
  • Number of fatalities
  • Whether hazardous materials (other than fuel) were released
  • All readily available information as to the nature and circumstances of the accident

FMCSA Reportability Triggers

An "accident" under 49 CFR § 390.5 means an occurrence involving a commercial motor vehicle on a highway in interstate or intrastate commerce which results in:

  • A fatality of any person
  • Bodily injury to a person who, as a result of the injury, immediately receives medical treatment away from the scene
  • One or more motor vehicles incurring disabling damage as a result of the accident, requiring the vehicle to be transported away from the scene by a tow truck or other vehicle
⚠ Accidents meeting any of the above criteria must be reported to FMCSA within 24 hours. Contact your safety officer immediately.

Post-Accident Drug & Alcohol Testing — 49 CFR § 382.303

As soon as practicable following an accident, the motor carrier must test each surviving driver for alcohol and controlled substances if the accident involved:

  • Loss of human life, OR
  • A citation under State or local law for a moving traffic violation arising from the accident, AND the driver received bodily injury requiring immediate treatment away from the scene, OR a vehicle was disabled and required towing

Testing windows:

  • Alcohol test: Within 8 hours of the accident. If not administered within 8 hours, document and cease attempts.
  • Controlled substance (drug) test: Within 32 hours of the accident. If not administered within 32 hours, document and cease attempts.
⚠ A driver who is subject to post-accident testing must remain readily available for testing. A driver who fails to remain available will be considered to have refused the test.

3-Year Retention Rule

All accident register records must be preserved for a period of three (3) years after the date of each accident. Records must be:

  • Kept at the motor carrier's principal place of business
  • Made available to any authorized Federal, State, or local official for inspection and copying
  • Retained even if the vehicle, driver, or carrier changes ownership
